# Competitor Scan: Web-based / AI-assisted custom jewelry design (Aurix)

Date: 2026-10-27

**How I picked the five:** "X" = web-based or AI-assisted 3D design of custom jewelry (Aurix's space). I chose the five most relevant products found in searches: Pencil, Star Gems GemArt AI, Kickflip, GemFind RingBuilder, and MatrixGold (the CAD incumbent). Other products seen but not profiled: Ring Facet ($70/month, https://apps.shopify.com/ring-facet), Keyideas Jewelith ($50/month), JewelApps Custom Ring Builder ($49-$99/month), RhinoArtisan, Diatech Studio. This selection is my judgment. [UNVERIFIED] that these are the market's "top 5" by revenue or users; no source ranks them.

**Marking:** Anything I inferred, or saw only in a search summary without opening the page, is marked **[UNVERIFIED]**. Everything unmarked I read on the page linked.

## What does nobody serve well?

All of this is my inference from the five products above. Mark: **[UNVERIFIED]** throughout, because I did not survey the whole market and the review evidence is thin.

1. **A neutral place that connects a designer to a craftsman who actually makes the piece. [UNVERIFIED]** None of the five does this. The configurators (Pencil, Kickflip, GemFind) sell designs on a retailer's own storefront; GemArt AI feeds Star Gems' own factory; MatrixGold ends at a CAD file. A craftsman-registration marketplace, as in Aurix's notes, was not found in any of them.
2. **Jewelers who are not on Shopify. [UNVERIFIED as a market gap.]** Pencil, GemFind and Ring Facet are sold as Shopify apps; Kickflip also supports Wix and WooCommerce. Small shops without a Shopify store would need a standalone tool. I did not research non-Shopify options.
3. **Dimension-accurate design without five-figure CAD training. [UNVERIFIED]** MatrixGold is $5,995 plus learning time, and its parent forum threads talk about months to proficiency (older source). The storefront builders offer preset options, but I found no evidence they let a user set exact dimensions. Pencil says "design tools for building your own pieces," which I could not test.
4. **Direct-to-consumer design. [UNVERIFIED]** Every product here is sold to jewelers or retailers and the shopper only sees the front end. None sells design directly to the end customer. Aurix's B2C angle appears open, but a consumer product would also have to solve the production step (gap 1).
5. **Pricing for small shops. [UNVERIFIED]** The ring-builder subscriptions run $59-$499/month and MatrixGold $5,995; no source shows what a shop with only a few custom orders a month would pay. Kickflip's reviewers complain about fees, and one says it isn't for stores under $10K a month in custom sales.

**Closest direct competitor:** Pencil. Its ring, pendant and nameplate builders match three of Aurix's design tools, it launched an AI jewellery CAD agent in May 2026, and its reviewers say STL exports are print-ready. **[UNVERIFIED]** whether Pencil supports earrings or accurate-dimension advanced design.

**Weak evidence caution:** The products with the best scores have almost no reviews (2-3 each), so the lack of complaints is not evidence they serve users well. The only well-reviewed product here (Kickflip) is not jewelry-specific.
